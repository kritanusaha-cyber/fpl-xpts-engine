Model audit · FPL expected-points engine

What this model is actually accurate at

Every headline claim recomputed from source, every tuned parameter checked for whether it was ever held out, and two previously published results that do not survive.

Updated 23 September 2026 132,305 walk-forward predictions Six seasons, plus five live gameweeks

The short answer

The model is reliably better at estimating a player's level and near-useless at forecasting a specific gameweek. It beats both reference benchmarks on mean absolute error in six seasons of six. Among players who actually start, its correlation with what they score is 0.064.

The claim that its selections beat a simple benchmark — which I reported earlier as the largest effect in the project — does not survive extension to six seasons. It reverses. Live, it does the opposite: over five gameweeks the top-ten selection beats the field in every single week, p = 0.012. Both results stand, and the sections below keep them side by side rather than quoting the flattering one. The live projection is running 24% low, and its ranking has improved in every one of five weeks.

Where the accuracy is

Three populations, three very different answers. Which one you quote decides whether this model looks strong or worthless, so all three are here.

2.33MAE, started

60.1% of rows are players who did not appear. Predicting zero for a benched player is easy and every method does it, which is most of what the headline 0.99 measures. Strip them out and the error more than doubles. Among players who started, correlation with actual points is 0.064 — effectively nothing.

That is not as damning as it reads. A starting midfielder scores two points most weeks; the gap between two and twelve is whether a goal happened, and goals are rare Poisson events nobody forecasts. What a model can do is get the level right — who plays, who takes penalties, whose team scores.

The live season now pushes the other way. Over five gameweeks of 2026/27 the top-ten selection beat the field in five weeks of five, by a mean of +3.64 points (95% interval +1.34 to +5.93, p = 0.012) — about 3.5× the field, the same magnitude the recent seasons give. This is the first live selection result in the project to clear significance. It is also the same quantity that reversed when the historical window was widened, on a sample of five weeks carrying a single test, so it is evidence and not a settlement.

Rank correlation rises in every one of the four steps: 0.458, 0.615, 0.687, 0.709, 0.746. A strictly increasing run of five values has probability 1 in 120 under random ordering, and the correlation against gameweek number is exactly 1.00. The mechanism is not mysterious. Gameweek 1 is a pure cold start with no current-season evidence at all; each subsequent week adds a round of real team sheets. It is also the size the historical fit predicts — the gameweek-1 blending weight of 0.65, fitted over six seasons, says one round of minutes outweighs a season of priors.

The top-ten selection beats the field in five gameweeks of five, by a mean of +3.64 points — 95% interval +1.34 to +5.93, p = 0.012. That is the first live selection result in this project to clear significance, and it sits at about three and a half times the field, the same magnitude the historical backtest gives.

It deserves one caveat rather than a victory lap. This is the same quantity that reversed when the historical evaluation was extended from four seasons to six, and five gameweeks is a small sample carrying a single test. The live data supports the claim; the historical data is mixed. Both remain true at once.

The top row still does not. A single pick returned 2, 23, 9, 2, 14 — averaging 10.0 on the strength of two hauls. Ten players is ten draws a week; one player is one. Nothing about captaincy has become knowable in five weeks.

Five gameweeks is still a short season. It is reported because a system that publishes its live record only when the record flatters is not being audited. What does not depend on sample size is the week-on-week improvement, which has a mechanism and a matching prediction from the historical fit, and the positional shortfall, which is large, flat and consistent.

The rank result is the strongest thing in the project: chosen without seeing a season and applied blind, it still wins that season, four times from four, at a cost of twelve points against the fitted version.

The incumbent blend needs a caveat. Blending a transferred player's own record toward the man whose place he takes beats using his record alone by 18.5% out of sample — but the per-fold optimum swings 0.95, 0.80, 0.40, and choosing it per fold is worse than a fixed 0.50. Fifty-five movers cannot identify that parameter. The direction is real; the value is a shrug toward the midpoint, and is documented as one.

One prediction from this table has now been tested forward. The gameweek-1 minutes weight of 0.65 says a single round of real team sheets outweighs a season of priors. That implies the projection should improve sharply after gameweek 1 and then flatten — which is exactly the live shape: rank correlation 0.458, then 0.615, then 0.687, 0.709, 0.746. A parameter fitted on six past seasons predicted the curve of a season it had never seen.
The remaining risk is concentrated in two places. The price-curve minutes floor was selected by moving it until the valuation split centred — fitting a parameter on the quantity used to judge it. And the fair-price shrinkage weight was chosen between two defensible options by which produced a usable spread, not by any test. Neither is validated, and both directly shape what the page recommends.

What auditing broke

Four defects surfaced while checking, all of which had been silently live.

  • The live projections were measuring a circle. Played gameweeks are folded into the cold-start priors, and the projection covers those same gameweeks, so the system was being scored on weeks it had already seen the team sheets for. Fixed by restricting the blend to strictly earlier gameweeks; error rose a tenth.
  • The scoring validation had stopped running. The live-season loader inserted rows with a null defensive-scoring flag, and the validator raised on the null rather than failing a check. The project's strongest guarantee — exact reconstruction — was not being tested at all once live data entered the warehouse. Fixed; it passes on all eleven seasons.
  • Two published numbers had drifted from their data. The captaincy figure was computed before a data backfill and never recomputed. And the projection log kept the newest forecast for a gameweek rather than the first, so each refresh overwrote the original prediction with a later model's hindsight view — gameweek 1 was recorded at 813 points, then quietly became 770. Both fixed; the log now freezes the first write.
  • Availability was a gate where it should have been a scalar. A player below a 25% chance of playing scored zero, and everyone at or above it scored as though certain to feature — so a player the game itself flagged at 75% carried his full projection. The cutoff was doing all the work. The stated probability now multiplies the appearance terms, which moved Palmer from 26.5 to 19.9 points over the horizon; injury, suspension and departure still zero outright, because those are not probabilistic.

What to trust

Trustthe level
Don'tthe week
Trustthe rank objective
Don'tthe captain pick

Trust it for who is worth owning over a horizon. Lower error than both benchmarks in every season tested, and the rank objective survives the hardest test in the project.

Do not trust it for what a given player does this weekend. Correlation 0.064 among starters across six seasons, 0.534 across all rows of the five live gameweeks. That is not a fixable defect; it is what the sport's variance permits.

Treat the selection claim as contested rather than settled. It fails pooled across six historical seasons, holds for 2023–24 onward, and now holds live in five gameweeks of five at p = 0.012. That is better than it stood in September and still short of proven, and an earlier version of this report stated it with more confidence than any of that supports.

Expect the projection to read low. Live it is running 24% under, flat across all five weeks rather than narrowing, and almost all of it is midfielders (1.37) and forwards (1.27) — the same bias the positional factors were fitted to correct, currently applied to squad selection but not to the number on screen. Five live weeks and six historical seasons now agree on this, which makes it the most clearly identified open defect in the model.
