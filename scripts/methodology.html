What this is

A Monte Carlo model of Fantasy Premier League scoring. Every player's projection is 20,000 simulated fixtures rather than a formula, because most of the scoring rules are thresholds — a clean sheet, a DefCon hit, the −1 per 2 conceded step — and the mean of a threshold is not the threshold of the mean.

Read this first. The 2026/27 season has no completed gameweeks, so every projection here runs on priors alone. Nothing has been validated against this season because there is nothing to validate against yet. The out-of-sample results below come from 2016/17–2025/26.

The scoring engine is proven, not assumed

The point values are generated from the live API's game_config.scoring rather than transcribed, then verified by reconstructing history from raw action counts:

Component models

Minutes — chained binary logits

Two models, P(appear) then P(≥60 | appear), rather than one ordered logit: selection and in-match usage have different drivers. Walk-forward over three seasons, 86,755 predictions:

ModelBrierSkill vs base rate
chained logits0.088155.4%
EWMA start rate0.112443.1%
last-match persistence0.120838.8%

Beating the base rate proves nothing — a decayed average already gets 43%. The model is 21.6% better in Brier than the best single-feature baseline, which is where the edge is. Calibration sits within ±0.04 across every decile.

Team goals — Dixon-Coles bivariate Poisson

lambda = exp(attack_h - defence_a + home) tau(0,0) = 1 - lambda*mu*rho mu = exp(attack_a - defence_h) tau(1,1) = 1 - rho

Time decay exp(-xi * days) with xi tuned on out-of-sample 1X2 log-loss; the optimum is a clean minimum at xi = 0.003/day. Fitting on xG beats fitting on goals (log-loss 0.9978 vs 1.0093).

Negative result, kept honest. Blending the model with de-vigged closing odds adds +0.00101 log-loss, t = +0.58, 95% CI [−0.0024, +0.0044] — not significant on 758 fixtures. Dixon-Coles adds no measurable value over the betting market for team rates. It earns its place only because bookmakers post no lines six gameweeks out, which is the horizon the optimiser needs.

Attacking returns — shares, not rates

E[player xG] = projected_team_xG x shrunk_player_share_of_team_xG

A per-90 rate already embeds the quality of the team the player was in, so multiplying it by team strength counts team quality twice. Shares are invariant to it. On the deployable comparison (market-projected team goals) this lifts correlation 0.3752 → 0.4622, +23%, and the gain survives into realised goals within every outfield position (MID +13.5%, FWD +13.6%, DEF +8.4%).

DefCon — hierarchical negative binomial

Log-minutes offset, dispersion varying by position. Action counts are overdispersed, so Poisson underfits:

SpecificationBrierBias
negative binomial, projected minutes0.0993−0.002
Poisson, projected minutes0.1010−0.028
negative binomial, flat 90 minutes0.1270+0.088
base rate0.11830.000

Evaluating at 90 minutes instead of projected minutes scores worse than the base rate. That is why the minutes model is built first.

Weakest component. Calibration collapses above p = 0.5: in the 0.6–0.8 predicted band the realised hit rate was 0.000. Samples there are small (37 rows), but that band is exactly where a DefCon-targeting strategy shops. Treat any DefCon probability above 0.5 as unreliable. DefCon contributes ~10% of a typical starter's projection, and ~9% of the largest mispricings — so the headline outliers do not rest on it.

Bonus — simulated BPS ranking

BPS is computed for all 22 players in each simulated fixture and 3/2/1 awarded on rank, so the goal–bonus correlation falls out structurally. Ranking by true BPS recovers 100% of bonus winners (760/760 fixtures) — the award logic is exact.

The inputs are missing. FPL stopped publishing key passes, dribbles, crosses, fouls and big chances after 2018/19. On 2016/17, the last season with the full action log: R² 0.9893 with everything, 0.8967 with only what the API still provides. The model recovers the bonus winner in 69% of fixtures, not 100%. The BPS weights used here are reduced-form and are not the official table.

Valuation

Value is a linear-programming reduced cost, not points per million — which flatters cheap players who could never make an XI:

surplus = xPts_H - lambda*price - mu_position - replacement_in_role fair price = price + surplus / lambda
  • lambda is the budget shadow price, from the LP relaxation of the squad problem.
  • mu is the dual on the positional quota. FPL forces a 2/5/5/3 squad, so you never choose a forward against the whole market — only against other forwards.
  • replacement is the median surplus among players who would make their club's XI at that role, so a squad player is not compared against a first choice.

Omitting mu made the metric structurally unfair: 76% of goalkeepers showed positive value against 1% of forwards, which said nothing about the players. With it, every role lands near 50/50.

Roles are clustered, not assumed

FPL's four positions are a scoring construct. "MID" holds both wingers and holding midfielders. Roles are k-means clusters on three axes — share of team xG, share of team xA, defensive actions per 90 — labelled by their dominant axis. The groups are recognisable: MID · high goal threat is the wingers; DEF · high goal threat is the set-piece centre-backs.

An earlier version of these labels was wrong. It guessed football names from cluster centroids and produced "attacking full-back" for two centre-backs, and put Haaland in "support forward". The clusters were real; the names were invented. They are now derived mechanically from the dominant axis.

Cold start for players with no Premier League history

187 of 592 players have never played in the league. Two approaches were tested on 244 genuine debutants:

PriorxG share MAEVerdict
position × price tier0.0297baseline
club-role inheritance0.0368rejected
Big-5 output abroad, calibrated0.0275 blendedshipped at w = 0.30

Role inheritance failed. The intuition — a new signing inherits what the man he replaces did — loses to the price tier on every metric, for two reasons: FPL's price already encodes the club's judgement of his role, and the role profile is minutes-weighted so it inherits the first-choice player's share. A backup striker was picking up the starter's 0.324.

Foreign output worked, after heavy calibration. Big-5 non-penalty output predicts Premier League share at corr +0.656, but:

xg_share = 0.400 x (npG90_adj / 1.45) + 0.0254

A slope of 0.40 means foreign output overstates Premier League share by about 2.5x even after a league-strength discount. Covers 55 of 187 signings; Big 5 only.

Leakage control

Every feature function is wrapped in a decorator that filters its input to gw < as_of_gw and then audits its output — a function can join its way back to the future, so checking inputs alone is not enough. Backtesting is walk-forward only; random k-fold on football panel data leaks the future.

Errors found and fixed

Listed because they shaped the numbers above, and because the same class of bug will recur:

  • Element ids are reassigned every season. Joining on them across seasons silently matched different people. This corrupted the newcomer test set (23 → 244 genuine debutants once fixed, reversing the headline result) and put Haaland in a centre-back cluster.
  • Position is a per-season attribute. Carried-over roles are discarded when FPL reclassifies a player.
  • Goalkeeper labels differ by season (GK vs GKP, with 2021/22 containing both). Unnormalised, every keeper silently scored zero.
  • 2022/23 has no xG before GW16, arriving as exactly 0.000 for 272 team-matches and being fitted as real goalless expectations.
  • Start rates were computed over appearances, not games available. A reserve keeper with one 90-minute outing scored 1.00, and three Arsenal goalkeepers all projected as near-certain starters.
  • Injured players counted as starters with 0.00 xPts, which made the "most overpriced" list simply a list of injured players and dragged role baselines down.

Known limits

  • Midfielders and forwards carry a measured residual bias of roughly −0.3 xPts per match, most likely unmodelled set-piece threat.
  • Gameweeks are simulated independently and accumulated: correct for the mean, mildly understating variance, since it ignores serial correlation in form and rotation.
  • Fair price is a linear extrapolation through lambda, which is the marginal rate at the optimum rather than a global price curve. It is informative about ordering and misleading at the extremes — a "fair price" of £17m is not a price FPL would ever set, it is a large surplus expressed in price units.
  • League-strength coefficients for the foreign prior are assumptions, not fitted values.
  • DefCon has one season of native data; its joint relationship with clean sheets measures ~0 correlation, but the doc's specified covariate (possession share) is not in the FPL feed.
